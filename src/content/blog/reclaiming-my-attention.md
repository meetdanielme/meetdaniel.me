Even though I now have AI do things for me across different apps, services and devices, my mind feels more scattered than ever. A big part of it is due to the sheer volume of information and content coming my way every day, and part is the number of things I consider important or just worthy of my attention. It's time I take back control over my attention and prioritise the things that matter to me the most and make me a better person.  
  
## Things that matter to me the most  
  
There are many things that fight for my attention every day, but some areas of my life are far more important to me than others and are worth prioritising:  
  
* Relationships with my girlfriend and my friends;  
* Staying healthy and fit (physically and mentally);  
* Expressing my creativity;  
* Learning and personal development.  
  
Overall, I want to be a good boyfriend and friend. This should take a priority over everything else. For example, I have been quite bad at responding to people I care about. Sometimes, it takes me days. Sometimes, I postpone it for so long that I forget to do it at all. I also delay making social plans until it's too late. I want to do better.  
  
I'm at the peak of my fitness abilities and have some running races coming up in the next few months. But I want to be more mindful of my training routine and consistency. I work out almost every day of the week, but I'm currently not too considerate about what kind of workouts serve me best in terms of my goals. I need more consistency here.  
  
Perhaps, my mental health is what needs the most attention, hence why I'm trying to prioritise the most important things in my life. One of the biggest challenges I face having ADHD is feeling overwhelmed. The constant noise coming at me from all directions is the biggest source of that, and I keep making it worse for myself. I love technology, but trying to stay up-to-date with everything going on across consumer products, Apple, and AI is no longer feasible with all of my other areas of responsibility.   
  
Moreover, I have neglected practicing mindfulness through meditation and journaling, which causes me to push my thoughts and feelings aside as I keep my mind occupied with the latest news or playing around with new tools and services. Also, I'm concerned that I leave too much of thinking and decision-making to AI these days. What AI is capable of is nothing short of incredible, but at the same time, it feels like it's been taking something away from me.  
  
Touching on what I just said about AI's impact on my life, I think it's been slowly stripping me from my creativity. Tools like ChatGPT and Codex have allowed me to do things I couldn't have imagined just a few years ago, but they also feel like a cheat code that I abuse. For example, I no longer let AI take over just the technical side of publishing my blog — I also let it do most of the writing and editing work based on very loose notes that I have. In work context, I think that's okay, especially since the content I produce in my role often covers industries I have zero to none experience working with. However, when it comes to my personal content, I should have an ownership of most of the creative process, with AI assisting me where appropriate. I want to draw a harder line here and not let my creativity slip completely.   
  
Somewhat related to that, I miss putting more passion into taking pictures. On paper, my iPhone has an incredible camera system, but I'm rarely impressed by the photos it takes. I want to try using Halide camera app more to reclaim the joy that I used to get when taking photos with my phone. And I don't reach for my FUJIFILM camera nearly often enough, which I should also work on.  
  
I took on university last September, which was the biggest shift in my life since graduating from high school. Reflecting on the first year at University College Cork, I believe I made the right choice, both in terms of going to school and the programme I chose. I'm ready for new challenges and finding myself in new kind of roles away from marketing, and Business Information Systems is getting me ready for whatever the tech industry is going to transform into with AI taking over. I should get the most out of the knowledge and the opportunities that the university and the course give me.   
  
I also want to expand my knowledge beyond the university curriculum so that I can truly immerse myself into the details of the work I might be doing in the future. I want to take advantage of free time I have this summer and expand my programming skills by learning more about Python, JavaScript, and picking up TypeScript, which, I believe, will make the coming academic year easier for me but also help better understand the output I get from working with Claude and Codex. I should priorities learning over jumping straight into execution, which I have a tendency to do.  
  
Beyond university and online courses, I also want to get back to reading books. I used to read a book a month on average. Now, it took me a year to finish the last book I have read despite my to-read list getting longer and longer.  
  
  
## Areas where I can take back from  
  
I need to take a real, hard look into things that make me feel overwhelmed on daily basis and reduce their impact on my attention so that I can prioritise the things that matter to me the most, including:  
  
* Social media;  
* Staying up-to-date on news;  
* YouTube;  
* Using AI;  
* Devices;  
* "Optimisation and performance mindset”.  
  
As most of us these days, I spend far more time on social media than I'm willing to admit, with Instagram being my biggest weakness. I tell myself that I keep it on my phone as a way to stay connected with my friends in Berlin and classmates in Cork. While that's not wrong, the vast majority of my Instagram use is just mindless scrolling with zero to little value back.

Trying to stay on top of the latest AI news made me get back on Twitter, as that's where OpenAI, Anthropic, and other labs share the most detailed updates on new features being added to their apps and models. Again, I don't feel like I get too much value out of it. Moreover, I feel like I'm trying way too hard to keep up with all of the tech news, which is really hard these days. I have been trying to keep it simple and stick to just catching up with my RSS feeds in the Reeder app, but even that feels too overwhelming these days. 

I have been also trying to use Reeder to fight with yet another weak spot of mine — YouTube. I use the UnTrap for YouTube browser extension on all of my devices to keep YouTube's distractions to absolute minimum, but that doesn't stop me from wasting time watching way too many videos on there rather than working on more important things or learning something new.

I touched upon my use of AI throughout the article already. While I don't think that AI is bad, I do recognise that it's impacting the way I handle planning or create things. For example, I used to put a lot of time into researching things to see when traveling and creating the perfect schedule. Now, I just let ChatGPT handle the whole thing and put the plan in my Notion database. While convenient, I feel like I gave up a part of the travelling experience that makes it feel truly personalised to me. I do want to continue to use AI tools, but I want to use it as a way to augment my own research and creativity process rather than have it completely take it away from me.

Moving from software to hardware. I feel somewhat overwhelmed with the number of devices I have and use daily. I have my iPhone with an Apple Watch and AirPods paired to it. Then, there are my personal and work MacBook Pros. Also my iPad Pro. And a Kindle. And Ray-Ban Metas. Also my camera. And that's just the devices that I carry with me (usually, not all together at once). While each of these devices has its specific use cases, I'm not great at drawing firm boundaries between them, especially when it comes to my primary device trio: iPhone, iPad, and personal Mac. I believe that each of these has its own unique, valuable strengths, I just need to get better at leaning into them. Having multiple devices also gives me an opportunity to create more intentional working environment, where each device has its specific role.
